ShiftReplay — App Map

Snapshot of the proof of concept after build revisions. Paths and symbols are checked against the source at the commit named at the bottom; the code can drift after that.

What it is, in one breath

One web page draws a container terminal with three.js. You pick a job, walk to checkpoints, and choose what to do. A wrong choice plays a short scripted replay of the accident; a right choice asks you to physically do the safe thing (hold a key, or time a key press). A report closes the shift.

Four pieces: the world (src/world.js, with textures from src/textures.js), the player (src/player.js), the content (src/scenario.js), and the runner (src/hazards.js) that turns content into what you see through src/ui.js and src/replay.js.

One action, end to end: choosing "Cross now" at the truck lane

Reference route (3 stops)

Labelled a reference route: it was prepared from the source, not toured interactively in this session.

Stop 1 — the trigger. src/hazards.js, method update(dt), branch if (this.state === 'walk'). It measures the distance from player.pos to the current hazard's zone and calls trigger() inside the radius. That is why the yellow ring is exactly where the decision appears.
Stop 2 — the content. src/scenario.js, constant CHECKER, object with id: 'cross'. The three options, which one is correct, which replay a wrong one plays, the action afterwards, and the rule. Changing a sentence here changes the screen; nothing else needs to move.
Stop 3 — the consequence. src/replay.js, branch if (id === 'cross' || id === 'report'). A dummy worker walks to z = 17.8 while a truck travels at v = 15 replay-metres per second; impactAt is when they meet, and the runner flashes the screen there. The camera path is cam(t).

A practice to reuse

Decision: keep all content as data (src/scenario.js) and all behaviour as a small state machine (src/hazards.js). Evidence: adding two more roles with six checkpoints (devpost/checklist.md > Slices > 4) needed no changes to the runner, and reusing a replay with new captions was a one-field addition (captions). Elsewhere: when an app is "the same loop over different content", write the loop once and keep the content in one file; the agent can then extend the product by editing data, which is cheap to review.

Verification used during the build
